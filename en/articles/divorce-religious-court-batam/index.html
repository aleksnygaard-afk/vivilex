How Divorce Works at the Religious Court in Batam: Step by Step

Vivilex Attorneys at Law · 20 November 2026 · 2 min read

Divorce is a difficult decision, and the process can feel confusing. This article gives a general explanation of how divorce between Muslim spouses is handled at the Religious Court (Pengadilan Agama) in Batam. For non-Muslim couples, divorce is filed with the District Court (Pengadilan Negeri).

Wife-initiated or husband-initiated divorce

  • Cerai gugat is a divorce claim filed by the wife.
  • Cerai talak is filed by the husband, as a petition for permission to pronounce the talak before the court.

As a general rule, the case is filed with the Religious Court for the area where the wife lives. A divorce is only granted on legally valid grounds, for example continuous disputes and quarrels that cannot be reconciled.

Documents usually required

  • The original marriage book and a copy;
  • ID cards (KTP) and the family card (KK);
  • Children's birth certificates, if custody is claimed;
  • Evidence of marital assets, if their division is claimed at the same time.

Stages of the case

  1. Filing. The claim or petition is registered with the court, including electronically through e-Court, and the court fee is paid.
  2. Mediation. At the first hearing attended by both parties, the court requires mediation to attempt reconciliation.
  3. Examination. If mediation fails, the case is examined: answer, reply, rejoinder, and evidence through documents and witnesses.
  4. Judgment. The judges give their decision. In a cerai talak, the husband then pronounces the talak in court.
  5. Divorce certificate. Once the judgment is final, the court issues the divorce certificate (akta cerai).

Custody, maintenance, and marital property

Claims for child custody (hadhanah), child maintenance, post-divorce maintenance (iddah and mut'ah), and division of marital property can be filed together with the divorce or separately. Agreements reached in mediation can be included in the judgment.

How long does it take?

It depends on whether both parties attend, the outcome of mediation, and whether children or assets are disputed. Uncontested cases are generally resolved faster. We give you an estimate after reviewing your situation.

For mixed-nationality couples

If one spouse is a foreign national, attention is needed to where and how the marriage was registered, recognition of the judgment in the home country, and the children's citizenship. We can explain this process in English or Norwegian.
